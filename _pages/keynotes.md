        <h4>Biography</h4>
        <p>Huazhu Fu is a Principal Scientist at the Institute of Advanced Intelligence and Computing (IAIC), Agency for Science, Technology and Research (A*STAR), Singapore. His research focuses on AI for healthcare, including medical image analysis, medical vision-language models, and medical foundation models, as well as trustworthy AI, with expertise in federated learning, uncertainty estimation, and visual grounding. He is a Fellow of the Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET), a Clarivate Highly Cited Researcher, and is recognized among the World's Top 2% Scientists by Stanford University. He serves as an Associate Editor for IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging, IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems, and Pattern Recognition, among others. He has organized numerous challenges at MICCAI on ophthalmic image analysis and has received multiple best paper awards, including at IEEE ICME and several MICCAI workshops.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="keynote-abstract">
        <h4>Abstract: Towards Trustworthy Medical Intelligence: Addressing Reliability and Explainability in Medical AI</h4>
        <p>Medical AI has demonstrated substantial promise in clinical applications, yet its deployment in real-world healthcare settings remains constrained by two critical challenges: reliability concerns and limited explainability. This talk presents our recent works addressing both dimensions through uncertainty-aware modeling and grounded explanation generation. These developments advance trustworthy medical AI systems that provide reliable diagnostic support with transparent reasoning processes essential for clinical acceptance and patient safety. Key contributions include uncertainty-based reliability mechanisms for out-of-distribution detection and hallucination prevention, and grounded explanation frameworks for enhanced clinical interpretability.</p>

        <h4>Biography</h4>
        <p>Chenliang Xu is an Associate Professor of Computer Science at the University of Rochester. His research focuses on multimodal AI, integrating vision, sound, and language to understand and reason about dynamic scenes. He has coauthored 150 publications, and his work has received support from federal agencies including DARPA, ARPA-H, NIH, and NSF, as well as industry partners including Meta, Sony, Adobe, and Cisco. His honors include Rochester's 2025 Edmund A. Hajim Outstanding Faculty Award, the James P. Wilmot Distinguished Professorship, and multiple paper and demonstration awards. He has served as an area chair for CVPR and ACM Multimedia and will serve as a Program Chair for WACV 2028. He received his Ph.D. from the University of Michigan in 2016.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="keynote-abstract">
        <h4>Abstract: Multi-level Alignment in Audio-Visual Generation and Learning</h4>
        <p>How can machines connect what they see with what they hear to understand and generate coherent audio-visual experiences? This talk examines audio-visual alignment at multiple levels, ranging from semantic categories and object identities to the geometry and physical acoustics of a scene. I will present our recent work on three related problems. First, I will discuss audio-visual scene synthesis, where neural scene representations and physics-guided generative modeling enable spatial audio synthesis at novel source and listener positions. Second, I will describe sound separation guided by visual or language cues, including how pretrained audio generative models can isolate sounds without task-specific training. Third, I will introduce language-guided joint audio-visual editing, which modifies visual content and its accompanying sound while preserving source characteristics and maintaining consistency across modalities. Together, these studies show how the demands of audio-visual alignment change across tasks, from identifying a sound source to preserving content during editing and modeling how sound travels through a scene. I will discuss what these differences imply for designing models that produce semantically consistent and physically plausible audio-visual content.</p>
